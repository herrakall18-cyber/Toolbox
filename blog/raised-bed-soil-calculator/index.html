Raised Bed Soil Calculator

Two weeks ago I was standing in a muddy garden supply yard, squinting at a phone calculator app, trying to figure out how many 1.5 cubic foot bags of topsoil I needed for three new 8-foot-by-4-foot beds. I had the dimensions right, but then I second-guessed myself: did I really want 7" deep or was it 9"? And I wanted a blend of 60% topsoil, 30% compost, 10% aeration. But none of that matters if the total volume is wrong.

I ended up buying 12 bags of this and 6 bags of that. I came up ~2 bags short at the end, and had to go back for them. That’s not a tragedy, but it’s exactly the kind of annoying friction that makes a weekend project drag. And when I filled the beds, I had a little leftover, but not enough to actually do something with. That’s how a simple task becomes “let me just stare at a spreadsheet” — which is easy to do wrong.

What do other calculators do? Not enough.

I tried three different online soil calculators that night. One asked for “apparent depth” and “void ratio” — that’s overkill for someone just filling a bed for tomatoes. Another one only gave me the result in cubic yards — which is fine if you’re ordering a full truck, but I was mixing bags. And the third lacked the one thing I actually needed: the ability to add multiple beds at once and get a bag count for different bag sizes. I have two beds of one size and one of another, and all the existing tools made me do separate rounds of math and then add them up myself. That’s what a tool should do for you.

So I built the thing I wanted.

What the tool actually does

The Raised Bed Soil Calculator lets you enter the dimensions of one or more raised beds — length, width, depth in feet or inches. You can also choose how many beds of the same size you have. Then you pick the bag size you’re buying (usually 1.5 or 2 cubic feet, but you can enter a custom size), and the tool does the rest. It tells you:

  • Total soil volume needed in cubic feet and cubic yards.
  • Exact number of full bags you’ll need — always rounded up, with no guessing.
  • Reference table for how many bags that translates to per common bag size.

It also handles the bulk option: if you decide to buy by the cubic yard, it shows you that too. No confusing unit switching — you see both the bag count and the yardage. For me, that’s the whole point: get a clear answer and go back to gardening.

And I made it add multiple beds without any extra clicks. You just hit “+ add another bed” and it recalculates everything live. No page reloads, no “how are my 4 beds entered?” — it just works.

Honestly, why use this instead of doing it in your head?

Because mental math is fine until you’re juggling three beds, a pickup schedule, and your kid’s soccer game. I’ve made the mistake of being too confident with numbers before. And 9 times out of 10, the cheap garden calculators give you something that looks right until you’re measuring an empty bed with a ruler and a growing sense of regret. This tool just removes the step where you second-guess yourself.

I use it every time I set up a new bed. Even when it’s just one bed, I’ll plug in the dimensions to make sure I get the bags right and avoid driving 20 minutes for a single bag of soil. I also added a “waste factor” slider because I always spill a little or some soil compacts — but it defaults to 0%, so you control that.

It’s a simple thing, but it’s the difference between a smooth Saturday and a trip to the store on Monday.
